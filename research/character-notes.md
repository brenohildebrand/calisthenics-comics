# Character research & mascot concepts — Calisthenics Comics
Compiled 2026-10-16. Hard constraint: **classic comic-book style stays the core** (bold ink, halftone/Ben-Day dots, panels, SFX lettering, cel shading). Authenticity comes from *who* the character is, not from leaving comics.
Fetched in full: Duolingo "Building character", characters.biz Design 101, Jonah's Luffy design essay, CNN Bartendaz 2009, WSWCF About. The rest come from search-result excerpts.

## 1. Character design principles
- **Silhouette test:** fill the figure solid black. One or two signature shapes (Luffy's hat, Goku's hair, Pikachu's ears) carry recognition. Test it in action poses and at thumbnail size.
- **Shape language:** circle = friendly, square = dependable/strong, triangle = dynamic/dangerous. Pick one dominant shape and one accent shape.
- **Colour:** 2–3 main colours plus 1 accent, and check the values in greyscale. A signature colour survives redesigns (Naruto's orange, Mario's red/blue).
- **Exaggeration:** push the trait that matters (Popeye's forearms, All Might's shoulders). Head count sets the tone: 2–3 heads = cute mascot, 8–9 = heroic.
- **Readability:** cluster detail at the face, leave rest areas, and add story details (a scar, a repaired item, a borrowed coat).
- **Mailchimp's Freddie:** simplified over years to cleaner lines, a stronger silhouette and less detail. He never speaks, only winks.

## 2. Comic-specific character design
- **Kirby:** strong anatomy first, then exaggeration. Rib cage and pelvis counter-twist, forced-perspective fists and feet, black "squiggle" shadow shapes, and Kirby Krackle (black dots whose negative space reads as energy). He spotted blacks to steer the eye.

- **Ditko (Spider-Man):** a full mask makes him a reader stand-in. With no face, emotion comes from awkward, contorted body language. A lean, gangly underdog, not a bodybuilder. Warm red-orange against cool blue.

## 3. Character writing
- **Want vs need:** the want is concrete ("could you photograph success?"); the need is an inner change the character denies. Chasing the want should expose the need.
- **The chain:** wound (one specific event, with an age and a place) → false belief, in their own words → visible flaw → secret → voice.
- **Backstory:** 3–5 formative moments, shown through behaviour, not exposition. Trauma isn't a personality.
- **Duolingo:** humans who are relatable but "endearingly askew". It deliberately featured cultures under-represented in Western media and kept all characters in one shape language.

## 4. Mascots in gamified products (Duolingo's Duo)
- **Geometry:** Duo was rebuilt from simple shapes (half-circle wings, pill feet) so he could be animated everywhere.
- **Emotion:** a full emotional range reacts to the learner's actions. The crying owl tested strongest.
- **Rewards:** animations are concentrated on rewarding moments.
- **Personality:** "pure and nice, but with a diva personality". The mascot owns the social channel.
- **Origin:** the green came from an inside joke. Specific, odd origin stories stick.

## 6. Street-workout culture and authenticity
- **Bartendaz (NYC):** founder Hassan Yasin served time, then used park bars for youth, school and Rikers Island programmes. "When I walk in the park and they're not chilling on the benches, they're at the bars." Camaraderie and smiling, versus the "dead serious" weight room.

## Takeaways applied to the concepts
1. Captain Rep fails the black-silhouette test: a mask and spikes make a generic superhero. A new mascot needs one ownable silhouette hook that also carries story.
2. Authenticity = a specific person in a specific place with an earned struggle (Rocky's Philly, Bartendaz's park), not capes. Brazil's praça bars are the real, under-represented setting.
3. Underdog > demigod. Effort must be visible (Kenichi), progression is the story, and that is exactly what a 1→10 level system sells.
4. Gamified mascots need a full emotional range and reward reactions (Duo). A simple, iconic face suits masking (McCloud). Big ink shapes, spotted blacks and halftone keep it comic (Kirby, Mignola, Spider-Verse).

# Three mascot concepts (all classic comic-book style)

## A. ZÉ BARRA — "Zero pull-ups, one rusty bar, no excuses."
- **Backstory:** José "Zé" Barreto, 24, is a moto-delivery rider from a São Paulo periferia.
  - **Wound:** at 16 he was laughed off the praça bar after hanging there like a wet towel. His belief: *"If they see me struggle, I've already lost."*
  - **Now:** he trains between deliveries on the same rusty bar, wearing his insulated delivery cube-backpack as a weight vest.
  - **Want:** a clean muscle-up at the Saturday roda (group jam) before his 25th birthday.
  - **Need:** to train in public, fail in public, and take the crew's help.
  - **Flaw:** impatience. He skips progressions, so every move page has Zé either learning the easier step or paying for skipping it.
  - **Secret:** he still can't do a strict dip.
- **Voice:** fast, self-mocking, mixes Portuguese hype words with English. Catchphrases:
  - "Bora!" (let's go)
  - "Tá pago." (workout paid)
  - "Level 1 is still a level."
  - "Skipped the progression. Paid in elbow."
  - "One more rep — the delivery can wait 30 seconds."
- **Visual spec:**
  - **Silhouette hook:** the boxy delivery cube on his back, a rigid square that reads in solid black from any angle. Plus a mop of curls held by a sweatband.
  - **Shape language:** square (dependable, grounded) plus triangle accents (sharp elbows, lightning-bolt tape on his knees).
  - **Build:** about 7.5 heads, lean and wiry, not bulky (Ditko's underdog). Long forearms are the exaggerated trait.
  - **Outfit:**
    - Faded tank top.
    - Rolled-up track pants with one knee patched.
    - Worn sneakers with mismatched laces.
    - Taped fingers.
    - A chalk handprint on the cube.
  - **Palette:** cube teal #00A6A0, sun yellow #FFC93C, tank red #E5262F, ink #111111, skin warm brown #9A5B3A, chalk #F4F1E8. The cube is original and unbranded (no app logos).
- **Why it's authentic:** delivery riders and praça bar crews are a real, everyday Brazilian street scene. The weighted cube ties the "can it be weighted?" data to his body. His arc *is* the progression ladder.

## B. VÓ FERRO — "Seventy-two, two knee surgeries, one human flag."
- **Backstory:** Dona Ferreira ("Vó Ferro", Grandma Iron), 72, a retired seamstress from Recife.
  - **Wound:** after her second knee surgery at 65, a doctor told her to "rest for the rest of your life." Her belief: *"If I stop moving, they'll start talking about me in the past tense."*
  - **Start:** she began on the free outdoor gym in her praça and kept going, from dead hangs to her first human flag at 71.
  - **Role:** she grades every move's level, and is the crew's mentor and judge.
  - **Want:** to beat her grandson at max pull-ups on his birthday.
  - **Need:** to let others see her rest days. Recovery is part of training.
  - **Flaw:** stubborn competitiveness. She refuses scaled options.
- **Voice:** warm, funny, merciless. Calls everyone "meu filho" or "minha filha". Catchphrases:
  - "Meu filho, that's not a rep, that's a suggestion."
  - "Again. Slower."
  - "Rest is training too — don't tell anyone I said that."
  - "Rookie today, Legend later."
- **Visual spec:**
  - **Silhouette hook:** a huge round grey hair bun pierced by two knitting needles, plus a stocky, square torso on short powerful legs. Instantly readable.
  - **Shape language:** circles (bun, glasses, round face: kind) over a square body (unbreakable).
  - **Build:** about 5.5 heads, compact and broad-shouldered. Forearms and grip exaggerated, with visible knee scars.
  - **Outfit:**
    - Floral house dress over black leggings.
    - Wrist wraps made from sewing tape measures.
    - Thick round glasses.
    - Old canvas sneakers.
    - Little chalk bag sewn from fabric scraps.
  - **Palette:** bun silver #C9CCD3, dress marigold #FFB000 with coral #FF5A5F flowers, leggings ink #111111, wraps #F2E6C9, skin #C68A5E.
- **Why it's authentic:** free outdoor gyms full of older Brazilians in praças are an everyday sight. The flaw-and-need (recovery) teaches real training wisdom. An older woman as judge subverts the musclebound mascot and stays fully comic, like a Kirby-grade grandma.

Action pose: holding a perfect human flag on a green park pole with a 'POW!' sound effect. Slower.', model-sheet labels, warm Silver Age comic print look with slight CMYK offset, original character, not resembling any existing hero."

## C. CARAMELO — "The stray who learned every move by watching."
- **Backstory:** a Brazilian vira-lata caramelo (caramel street mutt, a beloved national meme and symbol) who lived under the praça's pull-up bars.
  - **Origin:** he copied the regulars every day. First the dead hang (by his teeth on a towel), then planks, then, impossibly, a handstand against a lamppost.
  - **Wound:** abandoned as a puppy at that praça. His belief: *"If I'm useful and impressive, they won't leave."*
  - **Want:** a crew name-tag, made official by the Saturday regulars.
  - **Need:** to learn he belongs because he shows up, not because he's impressive.
  - **Flaw:** a hype-dog who over-performs, distracts easily and chases every new skill.
- **Product role:** the Duo-style reactor. He cheers when you level up, sulks when you skip, and howls at Legend moves.
- **Voice:** mostly sound effects and one-word captions. He never speaks full sentences (the Freddie rule keeps him iconic). Catchphrases:
  - "AU-POW!"
  - "WOOF. (that's a PR)"
  - "*sad caramel eyes* — you skipped leg day"
  - "AWOOO — LEGEND UNLOCKED"
- **Visual spec:**
  - **Silhouette hook:** asymmetric ears (one standing triangle, one floppy) plus a big curled tail, readable at favicon size.
  - **Shape language:** round body and head (friendly) with triangle ear and paw accents (energetic). Bipedal when training, quadruped otherwise.
  - **Build:** about 3 heads tall when standing (mascot-cute, like Duo), with exaggerated chunky forearms and paws taped like a street athlete.
  - **Outfit:**
    - Red bandana collar with a small chrome dog tag that's still blank (pays off his want).
    - Chalk-dusted paws.
    - A sweatband on one ear.
  - **Palette:** caramel #D99A4E, cream muzzle #F6E3C4, bandana red #E5262F, tag chrome #B0BEC5, ink #111111, praça green background accent #2E9E5B.
- **Why it's authentic:** the vira-lata caramelo is genuinely iconic in Brazil, a meme and widely loved. Stray dogs that live in praças are real, and his "learned by watching the park crew" story mirrors how street workout actually spreads (YouTube and park imitation). He's global and wordless for an English audience, with a Brazilian soul.
